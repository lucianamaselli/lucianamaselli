# Hi, I’m Luciana Maselli

I’m an applied social science researcher interested in inequality and how public policy can respond to it. My research asks who has access to basic services and social protection, how that access is shaped by where people live and how they work, and what evidence can help improve public decisions.

I enjoy working with unstructured data, from historical documents and newspaper archives to open-ended survey responses. My dream is to bring different kinds of data together to answer questions that no single source can answer on its own and use that evidence to help solve public problems. I love social experiments, especially field experiments that let us test ideas and see how they play out in people’s lives.

## What I’m researching

- **Sanitation in Brazil’s favelas.** In my master’s dissertation, I examined which factors are associated with the availability of sanitation services in favelas, with the aim of informing public decisions about where and how to intervene. In a separate project, I review and map the existing evidence on sanitation, health, and living conditions in favelas, looking at which outcomes have been studied and where important gaps remain.
- **Labor markets and social policy.** I’m developing a research agenda on informality, pathways into formal work, and how social protection can respond to changing work trajectories. I also contribute to a field experiment on access to gig work and exposure to inequality, working on survey design, data quality, and the analysis of open-ended responses.
- **Historical demography.** I turn historical Italian census tables into structured datasets and use text analysis to study how U.S. newspapers discussed fertility and population control.

I use quantitative and qualitative methods and build reproducible workflows in **R, Python, and Stata**. I’ve decided to start sharing my projects here, along with the questions that motivated them, the methods I used, and the code and data I can share.
